Fur Garment MOQ and Sampling: What Wholesale Buyers Should Confirm

Before you request a sample or place a bulk order, confirm these five points in writing. They determine your cost, timeline, and revision rights.

The Short Answer

Before sampling any fur garment, confirm five things in writing: MOQ (minimum order quantity), sample fee and refund conditions, sample lead time, revision rounds included, and bulk lead time. These vary significantly between factories — what you agree verbally may not match what is delivered.

1. MOQ — Minimum Order Quantity

MOQ is the smallest quantity a factory will produce in a single order. For fur garments, MOQ varies widely depending on the factory's production setup and the complexity of the style.

Factory TypeTypical MOQNotes
Large export factory100–500 pcs per colorLower unit cost, less flexibility on design
Mid-size OEM factory10–50 pcs per styleGood balance of price and customization
Small / boutique factory1–10 pcsHighest flexibility, higher unit cost
Umay Garment1 pcSame MOQ for sample and bulk

Low MOQ is particularly important for private label brands launching a new style — it reduces the risk of committing to a large inventory before testing market response.

Ask before sampling: "Does your MOQ apply per style, per color, or per order? Are there higher MOQs for custom dyeing or special materials?"

2. Sample Fee and Refund Conditions

Sample fees exist because making a one-off garment requires the same pattern work, cutting, and sewing as a production run — without the efficiency of volume. A reasonable sample fee reflects real production cost, not a barrier to entry.

What to Confirm

  • Sample quotation: Share the material selection, specifications and planned order quantity so UMAY can confirm the sample quotation.
  • Credit conditions: Confirm any sample-fee credit and its conditions in the written quotation before sampling.
  • What the fee includes: One physical sample with standard packaging. Custom labels or hang tags may be additional.
  • Payment timing: Sample fee is typically paid before production starts. Confirm whether the factory accepts PayPal, T/T, or other methods.
Watch out for: Factories that charge no sample fee but build the cost into higher unit prices — the math often doesn't favor the buyer on small orders.

3. Sample Lead Time

Lead time is counted from the date all specifications are confirmed and sample payment is received — not from the date you send an inquiry.

Delays most commonly occur at the specification stage — incomplete color references, missing measurement specs, or unconfirmed label requirements. The more complete your brief, the faster the sample.

4. Revision Policy

Most factories include one to two revision rounds in the sample fee. Beyond that, additional rounds may incur a new sample charge. Confirm this before your first sample is made.

What counts as a revision vs. a new sample?

  • Revision: Adjusting a measurement, changing a seam finish, altering a color within the same material — typically no additional charge within the agreed rounds.
  • New sample: Changing the fur type, switching from open-front to zip closure, or fundamentally redesigning the silhouette — usually requires a new sample fee.
Best practice: Approve a detailed tech pack before ordering the sample. The more precisely you define the garment upfront, the less likely you are to need multiple revision rounds.

5. What to Provide When Starting a Sample

A complete brief produces a better first sample and reduces total time from inquiry to approved sample. Provide all of the following before requesting a sample:

  • Style reference: A photo, sketch, or reference garment. Be specific about silhouette, length, and closure type.
  • Color reference: Pantone code, physical swatch, or color name from the factory's available range.
  • Size specification: Target measurements for the sample size, or a size chart if ordering a size run.
  • Material preference: Fur type (fox, raccoon, Mongolian lamb, faux fur), lining material, any special finishing.
  • Label requirements: Brand label wording, care label language, hang tag design if applicable.
  • Packaging: Poly bag, garment bag, box — and any specific requirements for the destination market.

Pre-Sample Confirmation Checklist

  • MOQ confirmed per style and per color
  • Sample fee amount confirmed in writing
  • Sample fee refund threshold confirmed
  • Sample lead time confirmed (from spec confirmation date)
  • Number of revision rounds included confirmed
  • What triggers a new sample fee confirmed
  • Bulk lead time confirmed
  • Payment method and terms confirmed
  • Complete style brief provided: style ref, color, size, material, labels, packaging

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the MOQ at Umay Garment?

1 piece, for both samples and bulk orders. There is no higher minimum for most styles. Custom dyeing outside our standard 14 colors may require a higher quantity — confirm per style.

Is the sample fee refundable?

Ask UMAY for a sample quotation based on the agreed materials, specifications and planned order quantity. Any sample-fee credit and its conditions must be confirmed in the written quotation before sampling.

How long does a sample take?

3 days from the date specs are confirmed and sample payment is received. DHL or FedEx shipping is additional — typically 3–5 days to most destinations.

Can I order a sample in a custom color?

Yes. Provide a Pantone code or physical swatch. Custom dyeing adds 5–7 days to the sample lead time for the first color. Subsequent samples in the same dye batch are faster.
