How to Order Custom Fur Fabric from a China Supplier: MOQ, Sampling & Lead Time Guide

Photographed garment reference. Confirm material identity, finished measurements, construction and commercial terms for the selected project in writing.

If you've been scrolling Reddit threads or messaging suppliers on Alibaba trying to figure out how to actually place a custom fur fabric order from China — you already know how confusing it gets.

"Is the MOQ 500 meters or 50?" "Can they do my label?" "Will I get ghosted after asking for samples?"

The answers depend on your supplier. This guide explains which photographs and swatches can start a discussion, and which material, measurement and commercial details still require written confirmation.

Whether you are a fashion label, coat manufacturer or startup, use the photographed garment and swatches only as references. Confirm the selected material identity, dimensions, quantity, destination and terms in writing.

Step 2: How MOQ Is Confirmed

MOQ depends on the selected material, color, dimensions, quantity and customization scope. Ask for these inputs to be reviewed together and confirmed in writing.

How to approach MOQ conversations with any supplier:

  1. Be specific about your product — "I need fox fur trim panels for hood linings, approx 20cm × 60cm each" gets a better answer than "I want fox fur"
  2. Show your roadmap — If you're ordering small now but growing, say so. Suppliers invest in relationships, and a buyer with a plan gets better terms
  3. Ask about mixed-material feasibility — do not assume different materials can share one MOQ or one production plan.
  4. Ask what is currently available — confirm the selected material, color, quantity and written MOQ; do not infer availability from an archive or old listing.
The bottom line: do not assume a universal MOQ. Ask for the selected style, material, color, quantity and customization scope to be quoted in writing.

Step 3: Requesting a Useful Sample

A useful sample request gives enough information to quote the selected material, dimensions, quantity, destination and evidence requirements in writing.

What NOT to send:

"Hi, can you send me some fur fabric samples? Thanks."

This tells the supplier nothing. It signals you haven't done your research and may not convert into an actual order.

What to send instead:

"Hi, I am [Name] from [Brand]. I need a trim material for [destination and product]. Please quote a physical swatch against the attached color and pile reference, identify the selected material in writing, and confirm swatch size, charge, current quantity, planned order quantity and shipping terms."

What to include every time:

  • Your brand name and what you make
  • Requested material identity, pile or surface direction, color reference and required supporting record
  • Swatch size needed
  • Your intended order volume (even a rough range)
  • Willingness to pay for samples

Request a swatch quotation based on the material and specifications, with your planned order quantity. Confirm shipping charges and any credit terms in writing before ordering.

After receiving samples, test these before approving:

The photograph documents seam construction; define the inspection method, sample size and acceptance criterion before treating a result as pass or fail.

  • Shedding — define the comb or brush, force, repetitions, sample size and acceptance limit.
  • Color fastness — use the contract or destination test method, sample scope and acceptance criterion.
  • Seam strength — define the test method, force or tolerance and acceptance criterion.
  • Label legibility — define the approved artwork, test method and acceptance criterion.

Inspect leather grain, button attachment, and buttonhole stitching when reviewing samples.

Factors that extend timelines:

  • Peak season: Ask the selected supplier to quote current capacity and add a written contingency to the project plan.
  • Spring Festival: Confirm the supplier calendar, material bookings and shipping cutoffs for the actual year.
  • Custom dyeing or panel cutting: Add a supplier-quoted material and approval stage to the dated plan.
  • Label & packaging production: Confirm artwork approval, production and delivery milestones with the selected vendors.

Step 5: Questions to Ask Before You Commit

Use this checklist when evaluating a fur fabric supplier for the first time:

About the Product

  • Which materials are currently represented, and what record supports the selected material identity and available quantity?
  • What pile heights and widths are standard vs. custom?
  • Which colors are currently feasible for the selected material, and which require a physical swatch or sample approval?
  • Can the selected material meet the required panel dimensions and tolerance? Confirm feasibility and terms in writing.

About Compliance & Certification

  • What certifications do you hold? (REACH, OEKO-TEX, ISO 9001)
  • Can you provide test reports — shedding, color fastness, fiber composition?
  • Which classification, permit and destination documents are required for the selected product according to a licensed customs broker or qualified compliance adviser?
  • What country-of-origin labeling can you support?

About Private Label & Packaging

  • Can the submitted label artwork, wording, placement and quantity be accepted in the written project specification?
  • Which submitted packaging format, dimensions, material and artwork are feasible for the selected order?
  • What written MOQ, charge and approval conditions apply to the selected label and packaging scope?

About Orders & Logistics

  • What MOQ applies to the selected material, color, quantity and customization scope?
  • What payment milestones and safeguards apply to this order?
  • Which Incoterms do you offer — FOB, CIF, DDP?
  • Which port do you ship from?

Common Mistakes First-Time Buyers Make

1. Requesting samples from 10 suppliers at once
You'll end up with a box of swatches and no clear evaluation framework. Pick 3 suppliers. Compare systematically on the same criteria.

2. Skipping supplier verification
Verify the business identity, represented premises and production responsibility independently. If an inspection is required, agree its provider, scope, date, evidence and acceptance points before payment.

3. Forgetting import compliance
Classification, permit, duty, labeling and destination-document requirements depend on the selected species or composition, finished construction and destination, and can change. Confirm the current requirements with a licensed customs broker or qualified compliance adviser before ordering.

4. Assuming EU labeling requirements are optional
Do not assume one label format works in every destination. Confirm the product classification, composition, origin, care and other required wording with the importer or a qualified adviser, then approve the final artwork before production.

5. Not confirming packaging specs in writing
Packaging surprises are common. Get dimensions, material, and branding specs confirmed in your PI (Proforma Invoice) before production starts.
