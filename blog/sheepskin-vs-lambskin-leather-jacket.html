What Is Sheepskin Leather?

Sheepskin leather is tanned from the hide of mature sheep. It has a slightly more pronounced grain, greater thickness, and higher resistance to everyday wear compared to lambskin. It is one of the most widely used hides in the leather garment industry, particularly for jackets, coats, and outerwear intended for regular use.

Because mature sheep are broadly farmed worldwide, sheepskin has a stable, large-scale supply chain. This keeps pricing more predictable across seasons — an important factor when planning bulk custom orders.

Key characteristics of sheepskin

  • Hide thickness: typically 0.6–1.0 mm for garment use
  • Surface grain: visible, slightly textured — ages well with use
  • Durability: holds up through frequent wear and standard cleaning
  • Weight: moderate — structured enough for biker and moto silhouettes
  • Supply: broad and consistent, suitable for large-volume OEM runs

What Is Lambskin Leather?

Lambskin leather comes from younger animals, typically under one year old. The hide is finer, softer, and has a smoother surface. High-end fashion brands frequently use lambskin for its luxurious hand feel. However, these same properties — fine grain, low thickness — make it more susceptible to scratches and punctures.

Supply is more limited than sheepskin, which can drive up material costs, especially when sourcing matching hides in large quantities with consistent color and grain.

Key characteristics of lambskin

  • Hide thickness: typically 0.4–0.7 mm — noticeably thinner
  • Surface grain: very smooth and uniform — premium tactile feel
  • Durability: more delicate; shows scratches more easily
  • Weight: lightweight — suits fashion and tailored silhouettes
  • Supply: tighter, with more variation in quality across batches

Which Is Better for OEM Orders?

If you are placing a custom order for a structured jacket style — biker, moto, military-inspired, or workwear-adjacent — sheepskin is the more reliable choice for the following reasons:

  • Consistent batch quality: Larger supply means factories can match hide color, thickness, and grain across hundreds of pieces in a single order.
  • Predictable cost: Fewer price swings per season allow you to plan landed cost and retail margin with greater confidence.
  • Better structural behavior: Sheepskin holds its shape through construction processes — stitching, pressing, hardware installation — without deforming.
  • Longer product life: End customers report fewer issues with surface wear, which reduces returns and supports repeat buying.

Lambskin is the right choice when the brand positioning is explicitly luxury or fashion-forward, the style is lightweight (bombers, shirt-jackets), and your order volume is smaller with tighter quality control at intake. It is not the ideal starting point for a first-time private label run at scale.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between sheepskin and lambskin leather?

Sheepskin comes from mature sheep and offers a thicker, more durable hide. Lambskin comes from younger animals and is notably softer and lighter but less resistant to long-term wear. For custom jacket production, sheepskin is generally the preferred choice for durability and stable cost.

Which is better for a custom leather jacket — sheepskin or lambskin?

It depends on end use. Sheepskin suits structured styles (biker, moto) where durability matters. Lambskin suits fashion-forward, lightweight styles. At UMAY we work primarily with sheepskin, which holds up well across OEM and private-label bulk runs.

Is sheepskin leather more expensive than lambskin?

Not necessarily. Lambskin can cost more due to limited supply and higher grading standards. Sheepskin offers a wider supply base and more stable pricing, making it easier to budget for bulk custom orders.
