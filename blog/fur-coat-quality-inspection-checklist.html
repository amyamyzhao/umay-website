Fur Coat Quality Inspection Checklist for Importers

Three checkpoints — raw material, in-process, and pre-shipment — and what to look for at each stage before accepting a fur garment order.

By Cathy, Jiaxing Umay Garment · Reviewed by Zoey, Sales Manager · More than 10 years of experience working with fur coat projects · Updated September 2026 · 8 min read

The Short Answer

Build the QC plan around an approved product specification, then define three checkpoints: incoming material, in-process construction and pre-shipment inspection. Name the inspection method, sample size, defect definitions, evidence and release authority for the specific order.

Why Three QC Stages Matter

Most quality failures in fur garments trace back to one of three moments: substandard raw material accepted without inspection, construction errors that compound through production, or finish-and-packing issues found only at the destination. A three-stage process catches each category at its source.

For importers placing orders from China, the practical challenge is that you are rarely on-site. The questions below guide what to ask your supplier — and what to verify through photos, video, or a third-party inspector.

Stage 1

Raw material incoming — pelt grade, fabric weight, color batch

Stage 2

In-process — stitch density, seam allowance, edge finishing

Stage 3

Pre-shipment — shedding, measurements, color, packaging

Start with an Approved Specification

Inspection can only compare the order with an agreed reference. Before cutting, identify the approved product or sample, material record, color reference, finished-measurement chart and method, tolerance by point, construction details, labels, packaging and defect definitions.

Stage 1: Raw Material Inspection

This stage happens before any cutting. Catching a bad batch of pelts or fabric at this point avoids waste across the entire order.

For Real Fur (Fox, Raccoon, Mongolian Lamb)

  • Pelt grade uniformity: Check that all pelts in the batch are the same grade. Mixed grades produce visible color and texture variation in the finished garment.
  • Root condition: Part the fur and inspect the leather backing. It should be supple, not brittle or cracked. Brittle leather causes shedding.
  • Color consistency: Compare the agreed sample set under the same viewing conditions. Record the buyer-approved visual or instrument threshold in the inspection specification.
  • Moisture content: Pelts should feel dry and pliable, not damp. Damp pelts develop odor during storage.

For Faux Fur & Fabric Lining

  • Fabric weight: Verify grams per square meter matches the spec sheet.
  • Pile height and density: Measure pile height against the approved sample.
  • Shrinkage test: Where relevant to the selected material, agree the test method, specimen size and acceptable change before production.
Ask your supplier: "Can you send photos of the incoming pelt batch under natural light, next to a color reference card, before cutting begins?"

Stage 2: In-Process Inspection

Place the in-process checkpoint early enough that construction problems can still be corrected. Record the checkpoint, inspection owner and evidence required in the production plan.

Stitch Density

There is no single stitch count that fits every fur, backing and seam construction. Approve the seam type, thread, stitch density and strength expectation on the sample, then photograph or measure against that agreed reference during production.

Panel Alignment

Lay the garment flat and check that fur direction is consistent across all panels — all nap running the same way. Panel direction errors are most visible on long-pile fox and Mongolian lamb.

Edge Finishing

Confirm the approved edge construction for the style and inspect it consistently. Do not replace the written construction standard with a universal rule based only on appearance.

Lining Attachment

The agreed lining should be attached cleanly with no unintended puckering or separation. Inspect against the approved material, seam construction and attachment points.

Stage 3: Pre-Shipment Inspection

Conduct this checkpoint on finished goods with enough time left for agreed corrective action. The purchase order should name the inspection window, release authority and reinspection process.

Shedding Test

Agree a repeatable shedding or loose-fibre check for the selected material, including the tool, pressure, number of passes and acceptance reference. Evaluate results against the approved sample rather than an unsourced universal threshold.

Measurement Verification

Measure the agreed sample quantity against the approved measurement sheet. Each point needs a named measuring method and buyer-approved tolerance; do not apply one tolerance to every garment and measurement point.

Color Consistency Across Pieces

Compare the buyer-defined sample quantity under the agreed light source or instrument method. Record the approved threshold and corrective action before inspection begins; do not substitute an arbitrary number of pieces or an undefined visual judgement.

Packaging Check

  • Correct care label language and content for the destination market
  • Brand label and hang tag attached per spec
  • Individual packaging matches the approved ventilation, moisture-control and presentation requirements
  • Carton markings correct: style, color, size breakdown, destination

Define the Sampling Plan and Defect Standard

An AQL number by itself is incomplete. The purchase order or inspection instruction should identify the lot, inspection level, sample-size code, AQL values, and the definitions of critical, major and minor defects. If the buyer uses another sampling plan, attach that plan instead. UMAY should confirm the requested inspection basis in writing for the specific order.

Sending Your Own Inspector

Agree the inspection stage, available quantity, factory access, documents and corrective-action window before booking an inspector. The buyer can nominate its own qualified inspector or inspection company.

Factory access, inspection scope, fees and reinspection responsibility should be confirmed for the specific order before arrangements are made.

Note: Build enough time into the agreed schedule for inspection, reporting, corrective action and any required reinspection.

30-Point Specification and Inspection Checklist

Copy or print this list, then adapt every item to the selected style and purchase order.

  • Correct style, SKU and specification version identified
  • Dated approved sample or reference identified
  • Material identity and composition record approved
  • Color, print or dye reference approved
  • Finished-measurement chart approved
  • Measurement method and tolerance approved by point
  • Construction, trims, labels and packaging specification approved
  • Incoming material matches the selected record
  • Pile, backing, pelt or surface direction matches the approved reference
  • Incoming color lot checked under the agreed viewing method
  • Lining, closures, hardware and trims match the approved references
  • Required material tests and documents are current for the selected lot
  • Material deviations are recorded before cutting
  • Pattern and panel version matches the approved style
  • Nap or fur direction is consistent with the specification
  • Seam type, thread and stitch density match the approved sample
  • Edge construction and seam allowance match the written requirement
  • Closures and hardware are positioned and functioning as agreed
  • Lining attachment and interior finish match the approved sample
  • In-process evidence is recorded at the agreed checkpoint
  • Finished appearance matches the approved sample
  • Buyer-defined measurement sample is checked and recorded
  • Agreed loose-fibre or shedding check is completed
  • Finished color consistency meets the agreed visual or instrument threshold
  • Closures, pockets, trims and hardware function as specified
  • Labels, hang tags and destination wording match approved artwork
  • Quantity and size/color breakdown match the order
  • Unit packaging and carton markings match the approved specification
  • Buyer-approved sampling plan and defect classification are applied
  • Corrective action, reinspection and release decision are documented

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes fur shedding and how do I test for it?

Loose fibres can have several material and construction causes. Define a repeatable method and compare the result with the approved material or garment sample; investigate any material deviation rather than relying on one universal pass count.

Can I send my own inspector to the factory?

Yes. UMAY supports buyer-arranged inspection. Record the inspection stage, timing, access, scope, evidence, fees and corrective-action terms for the specific order before booking.

What sampling plan should a buyer request?

Specify the sampling standard, inspection level, lot size, AQL values and defect definitions that apply to the order. UMAY should confirm the requested plan and any style-specific checkpoints in writing.

Do you provide QC photos during production?

Yes. UMAY provides garment photos, videos and photos showing measurements for buyer review. Record the required views and timing with the order. Wholesale buyers also receive social-media promotional images for the agreed project.
