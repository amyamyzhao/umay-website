Fur Coat Quality Inspection Checklist for Importers

Three checkpoints — raw material, in-process, and pre-shipment — and what to look for at each stage before accepting a fur garment order.

By Cathy, Jiaxing Umay Garment · Updated August 2026 · 8 min read

The Short Answer

A reliable fur garment QC process runs at three stages: incoming raw material (pelt or fabric quality before cutting), in-process (stitch density, panel alignment, edge finishing during production), and pre-shipment (shedding test, color consistency, measurements, packaging). Each stage catches different defect types. Catching problems early — before cutting — saves the most time and cost.

Why Three QC Stages Matter

Most quality failures in fur garments trace back to one of three moments: substandard raw material accepted without inspection, construction errors that compound through production, or finish-and-packing issues found only at the destination. A three-stage process catches each category at its source.

For importers placing orders from China, the practical challenge is that you are rarely on-site. The questions below guide what to ask your supplier — and what to verify through photos, video, or a third-party inspector.

Stage 1

Raw material incoming — pelt grade, fabric weight, color batch

Stage 2

In-process — stitch density, seam allowance, edge finishing

Stage 3

Pre-shipment — shedding, measurements, color, packaging

Stage 1: Raw Material Inspection

This stage happens before any cutting. Catching a bad batch of pelts or fabric at this point avoids waste across the entire order.

For Real Fur (Fox, Raccoon, Mongolian Lamb)

  • Pelt grade uniformity: Check that all pelts in the batch are the same grade. Mixed grades produce visible color and texture variation in the finished garment.
  • Root condition: Part the fur and inspect the leather backing. It should be supple, not brittle or cracked. Brittle leather causes shedding.
  • Color consistency: Under the same light source, compare 10 pelts from the batch. Maximum acceptable color deviation: Delta E ≤ 3.
  • Moisture content: Pelts should feel dry and pliable, not damp. Damp pelts develop odor during storage.

For Faux Fur & Fabric Lining

  • Fabric weight: Verify grams per square meter matches the spec sheet.
  • Pile height and density: Measure pile height against the approved sample.
  • Shrinkage test: Cut a 30 × 30 cm swatch, wash per care label, and measure again. Acceptable shrinkage: ≤ 3%.
Ask your supplier: "Can you send photos of the incoming pelt batch under natural light, next to a color reference card, before cutting begins?"

Stage 2: In-Process Inspection

Best conducted when 50–80% of production is complete, so defects can be corrected before the full run finishes. Request video updates at this stage if you cannot visit.

Stitch Density

For fur-on-leather construction, the standard is 10–12 stitches per 3 cm. Fewer stitches leave seams weak; more stitches can tear the leather backing. Ask your supplier to photograph a seam next to a ruler.

Panel Alignment

Lay the garment flat and check that fur direction is consistent across all panels — all nap running the same way. Panel direction errors are most visible on long-pile fox and Mongolian lamb.

Edge Finishing

All exposed fur edges must be lock-stitched, not glued. Run your finger firmly along every edge — any loose sections indicate insufficient stitching. This is the primary cause of shedding complaints from end customers.

Lining Attachment

The polyester lining should be attached cleanly at all seams with no puckering. Pull gently at the lining seam — it should not separate from the outer fur layer.

Stage 3: Pre-Shipment Inspection

Conducted on finished, packed goods — typically 1–3 days before the ship date. This is the final checkpoint before goods leave the factory.

Shedding Test

Press a lint roller firmly across the fur surface five times in different directions. Light pickup (a few loose fibers) is normal for natural fur. Heavy shedding — a visible mat of fibers on the roller — is a defect.

Measurement Verification

Measure a sample of pieces against your spec sheet. Standard tolerance for fur garments: ±1 cm on length, bust, shoulder width, and sleeve length. Measure at least 10% of pieces, or AQL sample quantity — whichever is larger.

Color Consistency Across Pieces

Line up five finished pieces under the same light source and compare. Custom-dyed orders are most susceptible to batch variation. If variation is visible to the naked eye, request a color measurement report.

Packaging Check

  • Correct care label language and content for the destination market
  • Brand label and hang tag attached per spec
  • Each piece in a breathable bag (not sealed plastic, which causes odor)
  • Carton markings correct: style, color, size breakdown, destination

Sending Your Own Inspector

Reputable factories welcome buyer inspectors. The optimal timing is when 80% of production is complete — enough pieces exist to sample statistically, and enough production remains to correct identified issues.

Alternatively, third-party inspection agencies (SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, QIMA) offer pre-shipment inspection services in Jiaxing. We can coordinate factory access directly.

Note: Schedule inspection at least 5 working days before the intended ship date. Last-minute inspection requests leave no time to fix issues.

Full Pre-Shipment Checklist

  • Pelt/fabric grade matches approved sample
  • Color consistency — Delta E ≤ 3 within batch
  • Stitch density 10–12 per 3 cm at all seams
  • All fur edges lock-stitched (not glued)
  • Panel nap direction consistent throughout
  • Lining attached cleanly, no puckering
  • Shedding test passed (lint roller × 5)
  • Measurements within ±1 cm of spec
  • Care label correct for destination market
  • Brand label and hang tag per spec
  • Packaging: breathable bag, correct carton marking
  • AQL sample quantity inspected and passed

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes fur shedding and how do I test for it?

Shedding is caused by poor pelt curing, damaged roots, or insufficient edge lock-stitching. Test by pressing a lint roller firmly across the fur surface five times. Minimal pickup is normal; heavy shedding indicates a quality issue.

Can I send my own inspector to the factory?

Yes. We welcome buyer inspectors and third-party agencies. Coordinate the timing before the ship date so any issues can be addressed. Confirm access terms in the purchase order.

What AQL level does Umay use?

AQL 2.5 is our standard. AQL 1.5 is available for premium orders — specify in the purchase order before production begins.

Do you provide QC photos during production?

Yes. We send in-process photos and short videos at key stages — typically at cutting, sewing, and pre-packing. Ask Cathy to set up a photo update schedule when confirming your order.
